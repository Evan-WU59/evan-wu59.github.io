Linguistics + AI

My research focuses on lexical meaning, metaphor, and multimodal understanding at the intersection of cognitive linguistics and AI. I specialize in developing empirical and computational research designs that make linguistic questions operational and testable. I combine corpus and multimodal evidence with linguistically informed experiments involving large language models (LLMs) and multimodal large language models (MLLMs), including model training and evaluation. These models serve both as tools for linguistic inquiry and as objects of study.

My research takes a corpus-linguistic approach, combining corpus construction, annotation, and mining to investigate patterns of use in lexical meaning, metaphor, and multimodal meaning, and relating corpus evidence to human judgments and the linguistic behavior of large language models.

Research interests

Representation and understanding of metaphor and nonliteral meaning by humans, LLMs, and VLLMs

How humans, large language models (LLMs), and vision-language large language models (VLLMs) identify, represent, and understand metaphor and other nonliteral meanings, including contextual interpretation, paraphrase, conceptual mapping, and inference.

Computational semantics

Lexical meaning, polysemy, contextual meaning, and semantic relations studied through operationalization, measurement, and computational modeling. This includes corpus-based and corpus-driven lexical semantics: usage patterns, collocations, constructions, register, and semantic change.

Multimodal semantics and the perceptual basis of meaning

How linguistic, visual, and perceptual information relate to meaning, using multimodal evidence to investigate semantic organization and perceptual grounding.

Digital humanities

Computational approaches to language, imagery, metaphor, and meaning in literary, cultural, and historical materials, guided by questions in the humanities.

A shared methodological focus: formulating linguistic and humanities questions as research objects that can be operationalized, measured, and analyzed computationally. Across these directions, I combine corpus construction, annotation, and mining with computational modeling and LLM adaptation and evaluation.
